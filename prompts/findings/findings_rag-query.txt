Our estimates indicate that migrants are more inclined to move to household and areas with characteristics compared to native counterparts. This is true for factors associated with socio-economics as well as social capital, labour markets and time. Taken together, our estimates do not lend support to the idea or theory that this theory matters for re-settlement and internal migration, but it does lends support that another theory matters. To the extent that we are able to account for other sources of bias in estimation, we observe no statistically significant difference between migrants and natives.
