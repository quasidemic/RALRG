Analysis is guided by general perspectives on migration that view differences between immigrants and non-immigrants and natives as the result of individual characteristics and post-migration conditions in the host society. The study focuses on on social and economic positioning.

The main hypothesis on explanation for settlement preferences, housing choices or internal migration is that immigrants differ systematically from the native-born population in key outcomes, and that these differences vary by time since migration and socio-demographic characteristics. A secondary hypothesis is that disparities are partly accounted for by differences in education, employment, and residence stability.

Theory highlights relevance of economic resources, social and human capital, communities, networks, labour market opportunities and discrimination.
