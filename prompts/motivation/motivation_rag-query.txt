Patterns of immigrant settlement and residential choice are central to understanding broader processes of integration and social stratification. Where immigrants choose or are able to settle shapes access to employment, education, social networks, and public services, and may reinforce or mitigate existing inequalities. At the same time, settlement preferences are influenced by a combination of individual characteristics, prior migration experiences, and structural constraints in the host society. This study is motivated by the need for broadly applicable empirical evidence on immigrant settlement patterns, using clear population definitions and population-based data. By examining settlement preferences in relation to socio-demographic and contextual factors, the analysis seeks to contribute to a more general understanding of how residential choices among immigrants are formed and how they relate to subsequent social and economic outcomes.
